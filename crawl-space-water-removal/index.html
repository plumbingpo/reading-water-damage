Crawl Space Water Removal Help in Reading

Water under the house is easy to miss. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Warning Signs

  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust
  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
  • Fiberglass insulation sagging or hanging down between the floor joists
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • An odor like damp soil that gets stronger close to the registers
How We Fix It

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Remove Standing Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Shifting Piers and Supports

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. Doors that stick and floors that slope upstairs are possible signs.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

What You Breathe Upstairs

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Termites and Other Pests

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Once the area is dry it becomes far less inviting.

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.
