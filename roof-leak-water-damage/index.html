Roof Leak Water Damage in Reading: What to Know

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. Water enters at a raised shingle, a split in the flashing or a worn skylight seal, follows the rafters and decking downhill, and finally lands on the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Catch the DripsSet buckets or pans under active drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way. Lay towels or plastic sheeting down to protect the floor.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneDo not climb onto a wet roof or crawl through a dark attic. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Photograph the DamageTake photos and video of the ceiling, the attic and any damaged belongings before you move or throw anything away, then call us to start drying.
Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A fallen limb or large hail that punctures or bruises the roof covering.
  • Nails that have backed out of the decking and left small holes through the shingles.
  • Roof valleys where worn metal or piled debris lets water get under the covering.
  • Gaps at chimneys, vents and sidewalls where the flashing has separated or rusted through.
  • A skylight whose curb or seal has dried and cracked, so rain gets past it.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Insulation in the Attic

For days after a leak, matted insulation sits wet on top of the ceiling and no longer insulates. Fiberglass can sometimes be dried. Soaked cellulose usually has to be removed.

Ceiling Fixtures and Wiring

Because fixtures are holes in the drywall, a leak often shows up at a light or fan first. Keep that circuit off until someone has inspected it, since damp wiring may corrode or short out.

Rafters and Decking

Repeated wetting makes decking, trusses and rafters turn dark, swell up and go soft. Decay here means less strength in what carries the roof.

Drywall Ceilings

Drywall that stays wet stains, sags and loses its strength. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • The ceiling texture and paint that the repair has to match.
  • How much attic insulation is wet and whether it has to be removed.
  • How many square feet of wall and ceiling drywall have to be removed.
  • The length of time the leak went unnoticed, plus whether mold has started.
