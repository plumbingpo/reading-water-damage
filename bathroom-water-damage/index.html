Bathroom Water Damage in Reading

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. In Reading, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray
  • Dark grout that never seems to dry out between showers
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • A brown ring or sagging spot on the ceiling directly below the bathroom
  • Baseboard puffing up or paint coming off near the shower wall
  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Leaking Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

Mold Behind Tile

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

Poor Ventilation

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

Rot Under the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

Our Process

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Cavities

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.
