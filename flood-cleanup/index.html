Why Professional Flood Cleanup Matters

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. Professional flood crews are trained in containment, personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitizing protocols that keep your family from exposure to pathogens such as E. coli, hepatitis and other waterborne diseases.

Improper cleanup also tends to miss moisture you cannot see, and within days that trapped water can cause a major mold problem. Our technicians rely on psychrometric calculations, meter readings and thermal imaging to verify that each affected spot, from the framing to the subfloor to the inside of the walls, meets accepted drying standards. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.

How to Prevent Flood Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • A battery-powered backup sump pump keeps water moving if the electricity fails in a storm
  • Seal foundation cracks and apply a waterproofing coating to basement walls
  • Extend your downspouts to carry rain from the roof out past the foundation
  • Have a backwater valve fitted to the sewer line so sewage cannot flow back inside
  • Clear leaves and debris from storm drains and gutters, which lets water drain the way it should
  • Regrade low spots so soil around the whole foundation falls away from the walls

When to Call for Flood Cleanup in Reading

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Roof or wall openings left by a storm, with rain pouring through
  • Appliances such as a water heater, washer or dishwasher spilling water across the floor
  • Soft, wet drywall or carpeting that is soaked through
  • A water line on walls or baseboards showing how high it rose
  • Cracked foundation walls, low doorways or window wells letting water inside
  • Dirty water rising out of floor drains or sewer lines into rooms you use
  • A sump pump that failed during heavy rain and let the basement flood
